Colours both waves by the state of a MACD read (28, 42 and 9 periods, on 4-hour candles). The wave values do not change — only their colour. Where the feed has no 4-hour history the waves keep their own colour, and the line under the chart says from when the colours apply.

A hidden divergence is the opposite disagreement to a regular one: price makes a higher low while the slow wave makes a lower low, or a lower high while the wave makes a higher high. It is usually read as the trend carrying on rather than turning. Marked on the slow wave, drawn as a hollow mark, and labelled unconfirmed.

The same hidden-divergence reading taken on the RSI line instead of the slow wave, and drawn where the RSI’s own divergence marks are drawn.

The same hidden-divergence reading taken on Stoch K instead of the slow wave, and drawn on the Stoch K line.

Draws buy dots from a −60 gate instead of the default −53: the fast wave has to cross up from deeper in the low zone, so fewer buy dots appear. Only the buy dots change; the counts under the chart stay on the default gate.

Display

A viewing choice for this browser only: it changes no reading. Everything the chart draws in green and red is drawn in blue and orange instead, which stay apart for the common kinds of colour blindness. Three shapes help as well: a sell cross is a ring instead of a dot, the money-flow row near the floor is striped where it is negative, and the two dark second-pair divergence dots are drawn lighter so they stand out from the background.

What is on the chart

Two different things live here. Below the line are objects the instrument itself ships switched off — turning one on shows it where the instrument draws it. Above the line is simply what you are looking at: hiding something takes it off your screen, it does not change what the instrument measures. Everything starts the way the instrument starts it, your choices are remembered in this browser, and one button puts it all back.

How to use it: click or drag across any candle. The price chart and the panel below stay locked to the same moment, and the legend in each pane’s top-left corner shows that candle’s values. The picture above (a list on a phone) names everything on the lower panel.

The candles
Price. Green closed higher than it opened, red lower. The thin line is the highest and lowest price reached in that period.
Order blocks
Shaded boxes on the price chart, off until you switch them on. Each is the last candle that went the other way just before price moved hard. A solid edge means no candle has closed through it yet; faint and dashed means one has. Only the six most recent per side are drawn.
Two bands sit near the floor, and they measure different things
The thin green-and-red row inside the panel is money flow: green while buyers are ahead. The teal-and-brown band below the SIGNALS strip is the slow wave’s side of zero. They look similar and are not the same reading.
Why some marks share a colour
The instrument uses one green and one red across several different marks, so a buy and a bull divergence are the same green on purpose. Tell them apart by where they sit and by the name in the table under the chart. Anything marked unconfirmed is drawn and labelled, but its on-chart placement has not been reviewed yet.
The last candle
The newest one on the chart. While its period is still open its values keep changing, so treat anything it says as provisional; once the period closes they are final. The line beside the price says which of the two it is.
The horizontal lines across the lower panel
Fixed marks on the scale, not readings — they never move, the waves move against them. The middle line is zero: the slow wave above it means buyers were ahead over that period, below it sellers. The dashed lines further out sit at the distances this instrument treats as stretched, and the numbers beside them are that scale, not prices. A wave reaching one says the move is unusual by the instrument’s own measure. It does not say what happens next.
